# Why orgchartr?

**In one line:** orgchartr is the lightweight way to keep *living* org charts of the **partner** organisations you work with, cross-linked to the Microsoft sponsors who own each relationship — with the data on your own machine, not in someone else's cloud.

If you've ever been asked "so what — why build this instead of using X?", this page is the answer.

## The problem it solves

Leading through partners means holding a lot in your head: who's who inside each partner org, who reports to whom, who *your* team sponsors, and the small but load-bearing facts about each person — the decision they own, how they like to be contacted, that their team is reorganising next quarter.

That knowledge usually lives scattered across slides, spreadsheets, and memory. It goes stale, it doesn't transfer when you bring someone onto your virtual team, and rebuilding it for a review is busywork. orgchartr's job is to take that load off you: one clear picture per partner that your whole team can read at a glance, and that stays easy to update as people move.

## How it compares

| Alternative | Where it beats orgchartr | Where orgchartr wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PowerPoint / Visio org charts** | Zero setup, everyone has it | Charts are *data*, not drawings. A reusable sponsor directory, filters, CSV, and one-click re-export mean that when someone changes role you edit a field — you don't redraw a diagram. |
| **Excel stakeholder lists** | Great for sorting and pivoting | Structure you can *see*: reporting lines and sponsorship coverage are obvious at a glance, not inferred from rows. |
| **Teams / Entra org chart** | Automatic and always current | It only shows *your own* organisation. It cannot model a **partner's** org at all — which is the entire point of orgchartr. |
| **A CRM (Dynamics, Salesforce)** | Enterprise features, built-in sharing | No licence, no admin approval, no data-residency review. Partner data stays on your machine, and you can be productive in minutes. |

## The data-sovereignty advantage

This is the argument that's easy to overlook and hard to beat: orgchartr stores partner-employee personal data **in a folder on your own computer**. Nothing is uploaded to a third-party service.

Putting the same information into a random SaaS tool turns it into a procurement and compliance conversation — where does the data live, who can see it, what's the retention policy, has it been approved. A local folder sidesteps all of that. You keep the data where your organisation already trusts you to keep work information (for example your corporate OneDrive), and there's no new vendor in the picture.

## Where it deliberately stops

orgchartr is intentionally small. It augments your notes and planning — it doesn't replace your CRM, your CMDB, or your account plans. It has no login and is meant to run on your own machine for your own use and your immediate team's, not as a shared enterprise system. That narrow focus is *why* it's simple enough to set up in fifteen minutes and simple enough to trust with the data.
